---
title: "Forms and dispatch"
description: "Validate inputs and connect an action to your application."
---

`OperationForm` accepts `operation`, `onSubmit`, `pending`, and `initialValues`. It validates required values, number ranges, and select options before emitting an `ActionIntent`.

```tsx
<OperationForm
  key={`${userId}:${operation.id}`}
  operation={operation}
  pending={pending}
  onSubmit={dispatch}
/>
```

Here `userId`, `operation`, `pending`, and `dispatch` come from your application. The key remounts the form when the user or operation changes; `initialValues` initializes local state and is not a live synchronization channel.

The emitted action contains `id`, `operationId`, and `input`. Your dispatcher must authenticate the caller, resolve the operation from a server-owned registry, revalidate its inputs, and execute it. Do not trust a browser-supplied operation definition.

Set pending while the request runs, then validate the returned presentation before rendering. Retain inputs on recoverable failure. Use the action ID for duplicate protection in your backend; the UI itself does not guarantee idempotency.
